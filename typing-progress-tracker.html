How to Track Typing Progress

Key takeaway: Look at trends instead of a single score. A useful typing improvement combines rising WPM, stable or improving accuracy, and regular practice over time.

A single typing test can change because of text difficulty, concentration, fatigue or familiarity with the words. Tracking several sessions gives a more realistic picture of your typing development.

Should You Focus on WPM or Accuracy?

Accuracy should normally come first. Increasing speed while making many more mistakes can create poor habits and interrupt your rhythm. A practical target is to keep accuracy consistently high while gradually improving speed.

What Is a Good Improvement Rate?

Typing progress is rarely perfectly linear. Some sessions will be faster and others slower. Look for improvement across several sessions or several weeks rather than expecting a new personal best every day.

Why Practice Duration Matters

One-minute sessions are useful for quick speed checks. Five-minute sessions are better for measuring consistency, while ten-minute sessions can reveal endurance, concentration and accuracy under longer effort.

How This Tracker Stores Your Results

This tracker uses browser localStorage. No account is required. Your saved results stay on the current browser and device unless you clear site data.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my tracker empty?

You need to complete sessions on the Typing Practice page first. The tracker reads the saved results from those sessions.

Does the tracker work without an account?

Yes. All statistics are stored locally in your browser.

Will my data appear on another computer?

No. Browser localStorage does not automatically synchronize between devices.

How many sessions should I compare?

Five to ten recent sessions can already show a useful short-term trend. Keeping more history makes longer-term progress easier to see.
